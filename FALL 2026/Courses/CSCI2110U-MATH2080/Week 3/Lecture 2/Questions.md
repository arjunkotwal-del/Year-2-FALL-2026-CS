### Q5 — Direct proof
Prove that if m+n and n+p are even integers, where m, n, and p are integers, then m+p is even. What kind of proof did you use?

### Q9 — Proof by contradiction
Use a proof by contradiction to prove that the sum of an irrational number and a rational number is irrational.

### Q11 — Prove or disprove
Prove or disprove that the product of two irrational numbers is irrational. (Careful — this one might need a counterexample instead of a proof.)

### Q29 — Biconditional proof
Prove that if n is a positive integer, then n is odd if and only if 5n+6 is odd.

### Q37 — Spot the error
Are these steps for finding the solutions of √(x+3) = 3−x correct?
(1) √(x+3) = 3−x is given;
(2) x+3 = x² − 6x + 9, obtained by squaring both sides of (1);
(3) 0 = x² − 7x + 6, obtained by subtracting x+3 from both sides of (2);
(4) 0 = (x−1)(x−6), obtained by factoring the right-hand side of (3);
(5) x=1 or x=6, which follows because ab=0 implies a=0 or b=0.

### Q41 — Proof involving an average
Prove that at least one of the real numbers a₁, a₂, ..., aₙ is greater than or equal to the average of these numbers. What kind of proof did you use?

## Extra practice (not from lecture, classic proofs worth having cold)

**A.** Prove that √2 is irrational, by contradiction — write it out fully yourself before checking [[Notes]] section 6.

**B.** Disprove this conjecture with a counterexample: "Every positive integer is the sum of the squares of three integers." (Hint: try small numbers systematically.)

**C.** Prove that n² is odd if and only if n is odd — try both directions, using whichever proof technique (direct or contrapositive) works best for each direction.
