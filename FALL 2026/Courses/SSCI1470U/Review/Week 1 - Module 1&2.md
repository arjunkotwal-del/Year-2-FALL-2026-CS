## What to review before Quiz 1 (due Sept 18) and Discussion 1
- The "science wars": modernists (science = culture-neutral, universal) vs. postmodernists/multiculturalists (science = inescapably Western)
- Bala's own position: science has "dialogical" roots — built from exchange between Europe and Arabic, Chinese, Indian, Egyptian traditions, not invented in isolation
- The Copernican Revolution as Bala's central example (Copernicus → Galileo → Kepler → Newton) and why it needed multicultural inputs
- Fukuyama's "end of history" (science as unstoppable, Western-born, will dominate globally) vs. Huntington's "clash of civilizations" (science as another front in cultural conflict) — both still Eurocentric in Bala's view
- Needham's Grand Question: "Why did modern science develop in Europe and not elsewhere?" — and Bala's point that this question is usually split unfairly (praising Europe, blaming everyone else)
- Ronan/Bodde case studies: how historians praise Greek contributions to science but blame Chinese/Indian/Islamic religion and culture for "failing" to reach modern science, despite comparable or superior achievements (compass, gunpowder, printing, algebra, decimal system, trigonometry, Arabic astronomy)
- The "inverse-Merton thesis" — Merton says Puritan values helped birth science in Europe; Ronan uses the same logic in reverse to blame Confucian/Hindu/Islamic values for blocking science elsewhere — Bala calls this a double standard
- Lecture slides — check for instructor's own framing/examples, since quiz may pull directly from there rather than just the readings
