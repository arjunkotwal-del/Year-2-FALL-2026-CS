## Bala Ch.1 — Introduction
- The "science wars": modernists say science is culture-neutral/universal; postmodernists/multiculturalists say it's inescapably Western and demand recognition for indigenous knowledge
- Both sides share the same wrong assumption: that because modern science *developed* in Europe, its roots must be *exclusively* European
- Bala's alternative: science's roots are "dialogical" — a long-running exchange between Europe and Arabic, Chinese, Indian, and Egyptian ideas
- Key example: the Copernican Revolution (Copernicus → Galileo → Kepler → Newton) — only possible by drawing on math/astronomy/philosophy from many civilizations
- Europe gets credit for the *synthesis*, not for inventing everything alone
- Fukuyama's "end of history": science is the unstoppable, Western-born engine of historical progress that will force everyone to converge on it
- Huntington's "clash of civilizations": future conflict is between civilizations with irreconcilable values — extended by Sardar to science itself ("the invisible science wars")
- Needham's Grand Question: "Why did modern science develop in Europe and not elsewhere?" — Bala rejects one-sided answers that praise Europe and blame everyone else

## Bala Ch.2 — Why Did Modern Science Not Develop in Civilization X?
- The question itself is loaded — no one asks why Renaissance art didn't develop in India; science alone is assumed to be the one true universal path
- Historians (Ronan, Bodde) study Chinese/Indian/Arabic/Greek science and consistently praise Greek contributions while blaming non-Western religion/culture/politics for "failure," even when the same civilizations made major discoveries (compass, gunpowder, printing, algebra, decimal system, trigonometry, Arabic astronomy)
- The "inverse-Merton thesis": Merton says Puritan values helped birth science in Europe; Ronan applies the same logic backwards to blame Confucian/Hindu/Islamic values for blocking science elsewhere — double standard
- Bala's conclusion: real answer to "why Europe" is that Europe was where multicultural contributions got synthesized (esp. via Copernican Revolution), not because Europe was uniquely equipped or everyone else was uniquely obstructed
