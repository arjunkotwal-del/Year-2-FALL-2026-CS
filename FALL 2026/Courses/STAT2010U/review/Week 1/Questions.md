# Week 1 — Questions to Review

## Assigned (Sec 1.2): 1, 3, 5 (skip 5b stem-leaf display), 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17

These are all **stem-and-leaf / histogram / frequency-distribution** construction and interpretation problems from the textbook. Since only the *solutions* are posted (not the question text), pull the actual question wording from your textbook (Devore–Farnum–Doi, Sec 1.2) before attempting — otherwise you're just reading answers.

What each is testing (from the solutions):
- **#1, #3, #11**: build/read a stem-and-leaf display, judge skewness, spot outliers by eye, describe spread relative to a "typical value"
- **#5**: choosing stem width (1 vs 2 vs 3-digit stems) and the resulting tradeoff in detail vs clutter
- **#7, #9**: frequency/relative frequency tables — computing proportions like "at most 5," "at least one," "between a and b" from a frequency table (watch inclusive vs exclusive wording)
- **#13, #15**: proportions from a frequency table + describing shape (skew direction, whether outliers are visible)
- **#17**: histogram interpretation — typical value, symmetry, bell-shape judgment

## Self-audit prompts (don't just check the solution — answer these first)
- For a "proportion of values ≥ k" question: why do the solutions often compute it as `1 − (proportion below k)` instead of adding up the upper tail directly? When is that faster?
- What's the actual definition of "large variation" used in #1/#3 (spread ÷ typical value)? Where does "typical value" come from if you haven't covered mean/median formally yet?
- Skewness direction: if the long tail is on the right, is that a **positive** or **negative** skew — and which side do most of the data values sit on?
